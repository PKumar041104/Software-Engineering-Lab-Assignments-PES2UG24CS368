## 1. Objective

To use Jira to convert functional requirements into Agile backlog items, simulate sprints, and analyse project progress through sprint reports and burndown charts.

## 2. Project Scenario

**Podcast Guest Scheduling & Outline Builder Project**

The project scenario from Lab-01 was continued in this experiment. The objective was to organize the identified functional requirements into an Agile product backlog, divide them into Epics and User Stories, estimate effort, prioritize tasks, and simulate sprint execution using Jira.

## 3. Jira Project Setup

A new Company-managed Scrum project named **Podcast Guest Scheduling & Outline Builder Project** was created in Jira.

The Scrum framework was selected to organize the product backlog, manage user stories, plan sprints, and monitor development progress.

## 4. Epic Creation

The functional requirements identified in Lab-01 were grouped into four major Epics:

1. **Interview Scheduling & Availability Management**
2. **Guest Information & Content Collection**
3. **Calendar & Notification Integration**
4. **Episode Outline & Production Preparation**

Each Epic represents a major functional area of the project and contains related User Stories.

## 5. User Story Creation

A total of nine User Stories were created and associated with their respective Epics.

Each User Story followed the Agile format:

- As a [user role]
- I want [goal]
- So that [benefit]

The User Stories represented the requirements and goals of the Podcast Guest and Show Host.

## 6. Product Backlog Prioritization

The product backlog was organized according to functional importance, user impact, and logical dependencies.

Each User Story was assigned a priority level and linked to its corresponding Epic.

The finalized backlog consisted of:

- **Total Epics:** 4
- **Total User Stories:** 9
- **Total Estimated Effort:** 33 Story Points

## 7. Story Point Estimation

Story Points were assigned using the Fibonacci estimation scale to represent the relative effort, complexity, and uncertainty associated with each User Story.

Planning Poker was studied as a collaborative Agile estimation technique.

The completed backlog had an estimated effort of 33 Story Points.

## 8. Sprint Planning and Execution

Two simulated sprints were configured and executed in Jira.

### Sprint 1

**Duration:** 25 August – 1 September 2026  
**Sprint Goal:** Enable the Show Host to create interview availability slots and allow Podcast Guests to view and book suitable interview slots.

Three high-priority User Stories were selected:

- Create interview availability slots.
- View available interview slots.
- Book an interview slot.

**Sprint 1 Summary:**
- User Stories: 3
- Planned Story Points: 13
- Completed Story Points: 13
- Final Status: All stories completed.

### Sprint 2

Sprint 2 focused on the remaining functionality:

- Guest information collection.
- Calendar and notification integration.
- Episode production preparation.

**Sprint 2 Summary:**
- User Stories: 6
- Planned Story Points: 20
- Completed Story Points: 20
- Final Status: All stories completed.

## 9. Sprint Monitoring and Burndown Charts

Jira's Active Sprint board was used to track User Stories across three workflow states:

- To Do
- In Progress
- Done

Sprint Reports and Burndown Charts were analysed to monitor the remaining workload and completion of planned activities.

**Sprint 1 Burndown:**

The remaining workload decreased from 13 to 8, then 5, and finally 0 Story Points as the three User Stories were completed.

**Sprint 2 Burndown:**

All six planned User Stories, representing 20 Story Points, were completed. Since the sprint activities were simulated within a short period, the chart showed zero remaining work rather than realistic day-by-day progress.

## 10. Reflection and Analysis

**1. Did your estimations reflect the actual effort?**

The estimations reasonably reflected the expected effort. Simpler tasks were assigned 3 Story Points, while comparatively complex tasks such as interview availability management, booking, and episode outline generation were assigned 5 Story Points.

**2. Was your backlog well-prioritized?**

Yes. Core interview scheduling activities were prioritized for Sprint 1, while guest information collection, calendar notifications, and episode preparation were handled in Sprint 2.

**3. How did your simulated sprint align with your plan?**

Both simulated sprints aligned with the planned workflow. Sprint 1 completed 3 User Stories worth 13 Story Points, and Sprint 2 completed 6 User Stories worth 20 Story Points.

**4. What insights did the burndown chart give about your team's capacity?**

The charts showed that all planned work was completed within the simulated sprint scope. However, because execution was simulated within a short period, the charts do not represent realistic day-by-day team velocity.
